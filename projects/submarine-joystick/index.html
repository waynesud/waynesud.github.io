Hall-Effect Submarine Joystick

Project Description

This project is a Hall-effect submarine joystick used in SUBC’s steering system for the 2024 International Submarine Race. Since SUBC designs wet-race submarines, all electronics must be waterproof. Using a Hall-effect sensor provides contactless coupling between the moving joystick and the electronics, allowing the electrical system to remain stationary. This simplifies waterproofing, especially under high pressure at significant depth.

Contributions

My primary responsibility was designing the Hall-effect sensor PCB. The board itself was simple, and was focused on generating clean analog signal output with basic supporting circuitry. I handled testing and calibration of the analog signal to ensure consistent and repeatable output across the joystick’s range of motion.

In addition to the electronics, I helped with the CAD design of the mechanical enclosure, including the sensor mount, joystick clamp, and the module itself. I also assisted with soldering, connector crimping, and waterproofing the PCB using a urethane coating. Finally, I helped pressure-test the enclosure to verify that it was airtight and met the required depth rating.

Reflection

This was the first PCB I ever designed! I am very grateful to the senior team members, since with their guidance, the design process went very smoothly. They helped turn the previously unfamiliar PCB design into something very approachable; and now it’s a skill I practice regularly. This project gave me my first taste of practical, real-world electronics design, and I have been hungry for more ever since!
